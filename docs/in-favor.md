### Operations revenue for Starkville Utilities

The requested power draw by the proposed facility would equate to roughly $1,000,000 (one million dollars) as revenue for Starkville Utilities.

### Most concerns can be addressed via restrictions in the special use permit
> ... a special use permit would have restrictions that could not be violated. We have several tools at our disposal including the requirement for another special use permit, a stop work order process that disallows any construction until the requirements are met, the ability to use our code enforcement as a monitor and legal remedy to violations. We can set those parameters to meet certain thresholds or else the megawatt usage is curtailed or removed altogether. You disallow any expansion that strains the grid, hence the three party contract with TVA and the City and the business. [^1]



### Location is in an industrial park
This park has 2 concrete plants, Southwire, Columbia Industries, Andritz, and an airport (and a new manufacturing industry yet to be announced).[^1]
This is a prime spot for an industry facility to be situated.


### A city should support businesses trying to operate a legitimate enterprise
Ultimately, a governmental body should not keep a business from operating on the grounds of it being morally objectionable (assuming that it is). 
If a strip club wanted to open in Starkville, it would not be our place to deny that purely based on morals. 


### Location is close to mayor's owned apartments
Mayor Spruill has said at a few meetings that an apartment complex (she may have said more than one; I haven't looked it back up) she owns is situated pretty close to the location of the proposed facility, and basically pointed out that she wouldn't be in favor of something she thought would reduce the desirability of those apartments.
