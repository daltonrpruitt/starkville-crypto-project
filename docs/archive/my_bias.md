During my upbringing, I was taught to be a good steward of our gifted planet. 
As such, ever since I was a child, I have felt compelled to pick up even the smallest bit of litter I see. 
I have shed literal tears over roadside litter on occasion, only bringing myself back into composure when reminded that sometimes garbage trucks accidentally lose things out of the back. 
And open-bed pickups, as well; though, that's not much better than throwing it onto the side of the road yourself... You know better. 

As a teen, I learned about the word "conservative" and thought, "Yeah, I'm that! I want to conserve our world and the environment!"
It did not take me long to realize that is not what "conservative" meant, and I have been immensely disappointed in many of those using the identifier since. In case it wasn't clear, I should have used "conservationist". 

As a young adult, I studied Chemical Engineering, worked in a paper mill, and continued looking for ways to make a difference. 
I found that most people working in a production environment don't really internalize the full results of their work. 
As in, their contribution to the cool things their company/industry does, but also their simultaneous contribution to the harm their industry does. 
There was a oil industry person that gave a talk that eventually revealed he was a climate-change skeptic, which is just unscientific and disingenuous at this point.
Back in the 1970's, Exxon knew that climate change was real and soon-to-be dangerous[^1], and still chose to promote confusion and skepticism in the general public and prevent the US (and other countries) from making actual progress towards solutions.

That experience instilled in me a need to contribute in some way to climate change action (in addition to all of the other cares I have about this world and its people). 
In 2021, during my pursuit of a computer science masters degree and in the middle of the COVID-19 pandemic, I started working with a group of students at Mississippi State University that were competing in the [Carbon Removal XPRIZE](https://www.xprize.org/competitions/carbonremoval), a competition meant to spur innovation and work towards effective carbon dioxide removal techniques and technology. 
Thanks to our work (of which I was the engineering lead), the team was awarded one of the $250k student awards to fund further research. 
The prize fund was $100 million, donated by the Musk Foundation. 

I say that all to demonstrate that I believe human-caused climate change is a real problem, with real potential solutions that we have to focus on. 
If someone chooses to deny it, they are lying. 
If someone chooses to ignore it, they are throwing away the lives of countless people, some of which are their own descendants (not that I care about the lineage of who I am fighting for, but they might). 
Unless we pull together fully and do everything we can to reduce current emission levels, invest in alternative energy technologies, and then work to substantially remove existing carbon dioxide, we and our future will suffer immensely. 
Increased evaporation leading to crop failures leading to starvation. 
Increased energy in the atmosphere leading to stronger weather events leading to mass casualties. 
Increased workload of air conditioning systems leading to power outages leading to heat-induced death during the hottest months of the summer, mostly affecting the elderly and other at-risk individuals. 
There are too many potential avenues through which our inaction can cause massive harm to people, yourself included, for me to sit back, throw my hands up in the air, and say, "That's someone else's problem".

I feel that I have constantly striven to work towards the betterment of others. 
I don't know how to "chill" because I see a vast array of injustices in this world, some of which are due to people that have long since passed us by, but perpetuated in the name of the status quo.
I may not use the most effective methods, but as I have been saying recently: Doing **nothing** changes **nothing**.


And finally, I have a child. 
I know many children. 
I see them at the parks, in restaurants, and at the movies.
I see them in the images of social media showcasing the atrocities countries wreak upon each other; though of course none of the leaders usually face danger.

...

They are quite literally the future of our society, world, and proof of our existence. Without them, there is no one else in our universe that would know we were ever here. 
 as the [ever-changing adage](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2020/04/29/tree-shade/) goes: "A society grows great when old men plant trees under whose shade they know they’ll never sit."
