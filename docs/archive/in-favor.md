# Arguments in Favor


## Tax Revenue for the City/County
From discussions with Mayor Spruill[^1], the annual ad valorem revenue is predicted to be:
- $55,000 to the city
- $100,000 to the school district
- $85,000 to the county


## Operations Revenue for Starkville Utilities

The requested power draw would equate to roughly $1,000,000 (one million dollars) as revenue for Starkville Utilities. This money goes to a utility fund and not a general fund, so it could only be spent on utility upgrades and not general city expenses.[^1]


## Ample Electric Capacity

Atwell and Gent (the engineering consulting firm that does work with the city) was contracted to do a power study to make sure the facility's power draw would not put strain on existing infrastructure, and they determined there was "ample capacity in the substation serving the load to serve it and retain ample for 10+ years of growth."[^2] The substation they are talking about can serve up to 50MW, and the current load is only 3-5MW used by Southwire[^3]. This leaves up to 25MW for other industries. 

Some other quotes from the Fact Sheet provided by Mayor Spruill[^4]:
- "The customer is proposing to be a demand response customer which means they will curtail or shut down during certain peak periods as requested by SU (Starkville Utilities) or TVA [Tennessee Valley Authority – where SU buys its power from]."
- "The customer will be responsible for paying for all improvements to serve the site which includes substation and distribution upgrades totaling $792,641"
- "SU/Rate payers will not pay for any improvements, obligations or bear any financial risk associated with the project."


## Water Usage Comparisons

Estimated approximately 20,000 gallons of water usage per day normally, with 30,000 per day in the hottest summer months. This is not a significant portion of our current sales (~1%), and it is similar to some industrial facilities and less than some recreational facilities.

From the Fact Sheet[^4]:
> SU sells approximately 2.7 million gallons of water per day so this new customer would add approximately 1% of the current sales. Starkville has plenty of available water system capacity to serve this customer.
> For comparison, a concrete plant uses between 10,000 – 30,000 gallons per day based on construction season.
> One of our industrial manufacturing plants uses 50,000 – 75,000 gallons per day
> New splash pad at McKee Park uses approximately 60,000 gallons per day when in service

Though there is significant water loss at Starkville Utilities per this [Dispatch article](https://cdispatch.com/news/starkville-losing-nearly-20-of-its-water-to-small-leaks/), some might argue that the utility revenue from this cryptomining facility could reduce that loss.


## Special Use Permit Restrictions

Many concerns can theroetically be addressed via restrictions in the special use permit:
> ... a special use permit would have restrictions that could not be violated. We have several tools at our disposal including the requirement for another special use permit, a stop work order process that disallows any construction until the requirements are met, the ability to use our code enforcement as a monitor and legal remedy to violations. We can set those parameters to meet certain thresholds or else the megawatt usage is curtailed or removed altogether. You disallow any expansion that strains the grid, hence the three party contract with TVA and the City and the business. [^1]


## Industrial Park Location
This park has 2 concrete plants, Southwire, Columbia Industries, Andritz, and an airport (and a new manufacturing industry yet to be announced).[^1]
Many would consider this a prime spot for an industrial facility such as this one to be situated, as the industrial zoning is theoretically intended to accomodate any noise concerns.


## Legitimate Enterprise & Morality
A city should support businesses trying to operate a legitimate enterprise. Ultimately, a governmental body should not keep a business from operating on the grounds of it being morally objectionable (assuming that it is). 
If a strip club wanted to open in Starkville, it would not be the city government's place to deny that purely based on morals. 

In addition, though cryptocurrency is often used for drug purchasing, scamming people, and human trafficking, so is cash and most other paper currency. As Mayor Spruill pointed out at Alderman Pochop's town hall, you could theoretically buy anything with the $20 in your wallet. 

Disclaimer, I have never knowingly used any cryptocurrency, nor would I claim to understand them. 
But [some claim](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/01/09/what-experts-say-about-cryptocurrency-bitcoin-concerns.html) that cryptocurrencies with a public ledger, such as bitcoin, are actually traceable, and thus are not a better substitute for cash for illicit purposes. 


## dB Levels at Puryear, TN Facility
Mayor Spruill and Alderwoman Sistrunk traveled to another facility supposedly operated by the same developers in Puryear, TN. They used a decibel-measuring cell phone app to check the levels, and they found them to be below the proposed noise limit (60 dB). Mayor Spruill has said she did not personally find the noise bothersome.


## Mayor's Personal Stake
Mayor Spruill has said at a few meetings that some apartments she owns are situated pretty close to the location of the proposed facility. She basically pointed out that she wouldn't be in favor of something she thought would reduce the desirability of those apartments or the recreational value of nearby McKee Park.
