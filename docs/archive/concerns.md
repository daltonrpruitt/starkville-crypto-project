# Arguments in Opposition

## Power Usage

The project is supposedly requesting a power draw of 30 MW[^1]. For reference, this [Dispatch article](https://cdispatch.com/news/crypto-mining-center-pitched-for-industrial-park-road/) claims that would make the facility "Starkville Utilities’ second-largest electrical customer behind Mississippi State University."

Many are concerned that this high power draw could strain the grid and may necessitate utility upgrades to recover the high capacity used by the facility. A grid study has been performed; however, it was not an independent study, and all utility companies involved stand to make lots of money from selling electricity and water to this facility, including Starkville Utilities and the Tennessee Valley Authority (who sells power to SU). 

The Tennessee Municipal Electric Power Association "is working with a site developer, which [they] declined to name, to identify sites outside of Tennessee with the infrastructure capacity to handle a mining facility," per the Dispatch article above; it is unclear if they will financially benefit from the facility being built in Starkville, but they did present at the 6/12 work session with TVA[^6].



## Water Usage

Estimated approximately 20,000 gallons of water usage per day normally, with 30,000 per day in the hottest summer months[^7]. The Fact Sheet states this is approximately 1% of current sales and points out other industrial and recreational facilities that use equivalent or more water.

However, per this [Dispatch article](https://cdispatch.com/news/starkville-losing-nearly-20-of-its-water-to-small-leaks/), an internal audit at Starkville Utilities "showed that in both 2023 and 2024 the utility lost roughly 45% of the water it intended to disseminate to customers, either because it never made it to a meter, to a bill or even into the system," including "losing 19% of its water to small leaks." Though the utility revenue from the cryptomining facility could help improve some of that loss, in the meantime that loss increases the water capacity portion that the facility takes up, since there's less water to go around.

Some residents are also generally worried that the massive amounts of water necessary for cooling data centers (both cryptomining and AI) will affect the amount of water available for citizens, especially during extreme weather events. For example, during a drought in [Fayetteville, Georgia](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/data-center-used-30-million-gallons-unnoticed-is-americas-ai-boom-quietly-pushing-local-water-systems-toward-crisis/articleshow/131068240.cms), citizens faced low water pressure while a data center got tens of millions of gallons for "free" due to faulty water hookups.



## Noise pollution

From the Fact Sheet[^7]:
> Customer has agreed to meet sound requirement of no more than 60 decibels at the property line.

For reference, "the average decibel level of human speech is estimated between 55 and 65 decibels" ([source](https://decibelpro.app/blog/how-many-decibels-does-a-human-speak-normally/)).

A more complete discussion of this and its implications follow.

### Cooling methods and their sounds

A very important component of any computer system is the cooling system. 
There are several different cooling systems that can be used, but the two of interest to us are:
- air cooling, where fans blow "cool" air over the computers, the air absorbs some of the heat from the computers,  and then the heated air is exhausted back into the atmosphere
- evaporative cooling, where hot air is circulated over some water-soaked structure, where the water cools the air by absorbing heat as it evaporates (this is basically how we sweat, too)

The cryptomining site in Columbus, MS uses air cooling, and you have to use a lot of fan power for that. The noise from that site is loud. 
While it is close to the airport, plane noise is at least not constant. 
I have not heard anything from nearby residents about it.

The site proposed for Starkville is supposed to use some form of evaporative cooling, which requires much less fan power to achieve the same level of cooling. 
Therefore, it should theoretically be quieter.
From the Fact Sheet:
> Proposed project has a cooling system/fans that recirculate water through a chiller system. Water usage will be “makeup” water to resupply the cooling system due to evaporation, especially during summer months.

However, that does not mean that there is no reason to be concerned about noise pollution.

Some people sleep with some amount of white noise, where others may need nearly complete silence to sleep. 

As stated above, the proposed site "has agreed to meet sound requirement of no more than 60 decibels at the property line".
So, for the human-hearing, this should be relatively tame...if you don't mind someone having a conversation next to you 24/7.

#### Infrasound

One of the larger concerns of mine is sound that is outside the [range of normal human hearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_range), so below ~20 Hz and above ~20,000 Hz (Hz = hertz, or "cycles per second").[^5] 

Frequencies below human hearing are called "infrasonic sounds" or "infrasound", and have been associated with potential healing ability when controlled appropriately and potential harm with chronic exposure depending on intensity and frequency ([article](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/3/1553)). They are also colloquially referred to as low-frequency noise (LFN), which some studies have associated with stress, heart problems, and other issues ([LFN review article](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/10/15/5205)). Some residents living near [data centers](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c93dnnxewdvo) have reported negative health effects. 

Since the widespread deployment of data centers and other LFN-producing facilities is a relatively new issue, there are very few studies that directly address their possible health effects. However, absence of evidence is *not* evidence of absence, and there may be unstudied/undocumented harms. 
This is such an interesting phenomenon, some [internet people](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bP80DEAbuo) are investigating it, so you know it's a big deal! 😉

While the proposed site is not the same type of data center as mentioned in these articles, the potential still exists for the facility to produce infrasound/LFN. 
As infrasound is not easily blocked and carries much farther than sound we can hear, it would have much further reaching effects than audible frequencies.
 

### Issues for animals

As shown in [this diagram](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Animal_hearing_frequency_range_logarithmic.svg/1920px-Animal_hearing_frequency_range_logarithmic.svg.png) of the [Hearing range Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_range), different animals have different ranges of hearing, with most animals being able to hear at much higher frequencies than humans can. 
As such, there are certain sounds that animals can hear that we can't; this is the idea behind dog whistles. 

It is possible that the proposed site could produce high-frequency noise inaudible to us. Thus, it is important that such sounds be mitigated to avoid distress to local wildlife and residents of the Oktibbeha County Humane Society (OCHS), since the facility would be situated almost across the street from OCHS and right next to a forest.

Humans are just animals, so the concerns of infrasound/LFN also apply to the animals in the shelter and local wildlife.

## Lack of tangible goods/service and jobs

While mining cryptocurrencies does make money for the owner of the mining operation, no tangible product or service is made for regular citizens. 
This is a negative scenario when the power could be used for more productive means, as the "product" of this facility is not contributing to the welfare of the community in the same way that a concrete plant or other tangible industry might.

This cryptomining facility will also not create many jobs despite its massive power draw, with Mayor Spruill stating only 4-5 permanent jobs would be produced. Many would argue that it would be better for that power draw to go to industries that employ more of our citizens.


## Lack of developer/company transparency

The identity of the developer(s) has been elusive.

In the 6/12 work session neither TVA nor TMEPA directly stated who the developer is, only that they have a similar facility in Puryear, TN, and more under construction in Humbolt, TN, and at least 4 other locations.

For a while, all that was known about the facility was the involvement of [Starkville Energy LLC](https://opencorporates.com/companies/us_ms/1493520), which led to the facility being termed the "Starkville Energy Project" by some people. It's possible this name was chosen to give the appearance of local involvement/support.

Citizens concerned about transparency have done some digging and found the following:

- Per Mayor Spruill, the company listed on the land parcel is [Waring Investments, Inc.](https://opencorporates.com/companies/us_ms/584853); note this company uses NAIC code "Lessors of Nonresidential Buildings."
- Per public documents, the Puryear, TN facility is owned by [TN Wall Street LLC](https://opencorporates.com/companies/us_tn/001621831) and/or [Puryear Electric LLC](https://opencorporates.com/companies/us_tn/001561999). (This parent company seems to like naming their LLCs after the city they're building in.)
- Per public documents, the Puryear facility is subleased by TN Wall Street LLC and/or Puryear Electric LLC to [Old Const LLC](https://opencorporates.com/companies/us_tn/001597284), "which is charged with finding space/secure electricity around the country for clients that want to run their mining computers."
- Per public documents, Old Const LLC's client in Puryear is/was [Bitmain Technologies Georgia Limited](https://opencorporates.com/companies/us_tn/001545872)
- Per the names of their directors/officers, it is possible that at least either Old Const LLC or Bitmain are Chinese companies with a [U.S. domestic entity](https://www.sillscummis.com/considerations-for-chinese-companies-doing-business-in-the-united-states/)

To summarize (since these nesting doll companies get confusing), the developer that wants to build the cryptomining facility in Starkville owns another mine in Puryear, TN. That mine seems to be leased to another company (Old Const LLC), who then seems to have leased to another company (Bitmain).

The level of obfuscation here is concerning and does not bode well for enforcement of violations such as noise complaints.


## Possible desired expansion

Many data center projects eventually want to expand their facilities, leading to further increases in power/water draw and noise. 

Depending on the crypto coins mined by this facility, they may need to increase computer power as time goes on to accomodate the increasing amount of compute necessary to mine coins. Bitcoin in particular requires more energy as more coins are mined.


## Possible short lifetime of project

Given the volatile nature of cryptocurrency, it is possible for the companies that own this facility to rapidly go under and desire to sell to another developer that may be less willing to honor any agreements made. 

As an example close to home, the crypto mining facility in Columbus, MS was [sold in September 2025](https://greenidge.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Greenidge-Generation-Closes-Sale-of-its-Mississippi-Bitcoin-Mining-Facility.pdf). It is also possibly pivoting to [artificial intelligence](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/lm-funding-to-pivot-bitcoin-mining-sites-to-ai-and-hpc/) after "a loss-making first quarter for the company."


## Possible pivot to larger-scale data center

Bringing back up the facility in Columbus, MS, the facility was sold to a subsidiary of the previous owner's competitor, [LM Funding America](https://www.power-compute.com/investors/news-events/press-releases/detail/191/lm-funding-announces-corporate-name-change-to-powercompute), which has recently undergone a name change to Power Compute, "[reflecting strategic focus on high-performance computing and AI infrastructure and BTC mining](https://www.power-compute.com/investors/news-events/press-releases/detail/191/lm-funding-announces-corporate-name-change-to-powercompute)".

While that company is not the same as the one requesting to build in Starkville, it is prudent to point out the potential interest of companies to mine cryptocurrency while profitable but then pivot to a more general type of computing load when cryptocurrency is no longer profitable.


## This isn't just about our own health and safety - environmental concerns

The production of power in the U.S. is still heavily dependent on non-renewable/non-clean energy sources, such as coal, natural gas, oil, and others. 
While nuclear is technically non-renewable, it can be counted as "clean" in the sense that not as many emissions are generated during it's operation (and even when you amortize the construction emissions it is still much better than fossil fuel emissions[^4]),. 

Based on [TVA's Draft Integrated Resource Plan for 2025](https://www.tva.com/environment/draft-2025-integrated-resource-plan), they included a Draft [Environmental Impact Statement](https://tva-azr-eastus-cdn-ep-tvawcm-prd.azureedge.net/cdn-tvawcma/docs/default-source/environment/environmental-stewardship/integrated-resource-plan/2025/draft-2025-environmental-impact-statement.pdf?sfvrsn=d923248c_1) that gives the following table for 2020-2023 generation by type of production:

![](images/tva_generation_by_type.png)

Based on this table, I worked out that the "non-clean" to "clean" power production is as follows:

| Generation Category | FY 2023 | FY 2022 | FY 2021 | FY 2020 |
| ------------------- | ------- | ------- | ------- | ------- |
| Non-renewable       | 44.93%  | 47.36%  | 43.74%  | 42.23%  |
| Renewable/”Clean”   | 55.07%  | 52.64%  | 56.26%  | 57.77%  |

Again, I am counting nuclear as "clean". 
Based on this, we can see that non-renewables have made up approximately 45% of the power generation of TVA. 

At that rate, to generate 1 ton (2000 lbs) of CO2e would take between approximately 4.7 and 6.4 minutes, and that does not account for transfer losses and other minor inefficiencies. 

With respect to the crypto mining facility, this power breakdown means that it would emit approximately 68,000 tons of CO2e per year, which is approximately equivalent to the CO2e emitted by 7,500 homes in one year.

These emissions may not be happening here in Starkville, but they are happening in the communities that house these polluting power facilities. This pollution can lead to a variety of health effects such as asthma and premature births. TVA has also [underestimated the amount of emissions](https://wpln.org/post/from-nose-bleeds-to-cancer-the-public-health-risks-of-tvas-gas-buildout/) their own facilities are producing.
